# Credit Not Processed (Visa 13.6 and equivalents)

This category covers claims where a customer returned an item or cancelled a service and believes they are owed a refund that was never issued. On Visa this commonly falls under reason code 13.6 (Credit Not Processed); confirm the exact code on the dashboard.

## What the Issuer Is Actually Checking

Two separate questions determine the outcome: (1) was a refund actually owed under the merchant's own policy, and (2) if so, was it actually processed. A merchant can win even if a refund was warranted, as long as it can show the refund was in fact issued -- and can also win if it can show no refund was ever owed under a policy the customer agreed to.

## Evidence That Actually Moves the Needle

- **Proof the refund was issued**, specifically a transaction/settlement record showing a credit to the *original payment method* for the disputed amount. A refund issued to a different instrument, as store credit, or as a partial amount not matching the dispute does not fully rebut the claim.
- **The cancellation/refund policy as it existed at checkout**, ideally with proof the customer affirmatively agreed to it (checkbox, terms link clicked, order confirmation referencing the policy).
- **Communication showing a refund was denied for a stated reason** consistent with that policy -- e.g. a return window had expired, the item was non-refundable, or the cancellation didn't meet the policy's notice period.
- **Timing records**: refund policies often specify a processing window (e.g. "refunds within 7 business days"); if the refund was issued within that window, include the dates explicitly, since customers sometimes dispute simply because a refund had not yet appeared on their statement.

## Common Mistakes That Lose This Category

- Showing that a refund was *initiated* in the merchant's own system without evidence it actually *settled* to the customer's payment method -- issuers want proof of the credit landing, not just an internal status flag.
- Citing a refund policy that was not actually presented to the customer at the time of their specific purchase (e.g. a policy added to the website after the order date).
- Refunding the wrong amount (e.g. excluding shipping when the policy promised a full refund) and not addressing the discrepancy in the representment.

## A Note on Timing Disputes

A meaningful fraction of "credit not processed" disputes are filed by customers who simply haven't noticed the refund yet, since bank-side credit postings can lag 2-5 business days behind the merchant's own refund confirmation. Including the exact refund transaction date and amount is often enough to resolve these without further escalation.
