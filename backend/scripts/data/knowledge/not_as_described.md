# Services or Merchandise Not as Described (Visa 13.3 and equivalents)

This category covers claims that the product was defective, damaged, significantly different from its description, or otherwise not what the customer expected. On Visa this commonly falls under reason code 13.3 (Not as Described or Defective Merchandise/Services); confirm the exact code on the dashboard.

## What Makes This Category Hard

Unlike fraud or non-receipt, "not as described" disputes often involve a genuine difference of opinion about subjective quality, not a clear-cut factual question. Issuers generally side with the customer unless the merchant can show the listing was accurate and the customer's own behavior is inconsistent with their claim.

## Evidence That Actually Moves the Needle

- **The exact listing/description shown to the customer at time of purchase**, not a current version of the product page which may have since changed. Screenshots or archived snapshots dated at or before the order date are strongest.
- **Product images matching what was actually shipped**, ideally with a serial/batch number or order-specific photo if the merchant's fulfillment process captures one.
- **Customer communication logs** -- especially any message where the customer acknowledges receiving the item, describes it accurately, or negotiates a partial resolution, which undercuts a later "not as described" claim.
- **Proof the item was not returned**, or that a return was processed strictly according to the merchant's published return policy (which the customer agreed to at checkout). A customer who kept the item while also claiming it was unusable is a strong signal for the merchant.

## Common Mistakes That Lose This Category

- Submitting a *current* product page URL instead of the description as it existed on the order date -- issuers frequently reject this as unverifiable.
- No communication trail at all, leaving only the merchant's word against the customer's.
- Ignoring that the customer used the product for a meaningful period before disputing -- this is worth stating explicitly in the representment, since issuers weigh usage duration against a "defective on arrival" claim.

## Distinguishing From Buyer's Remorse

A genuine "not as described" dispute differs from a customer simply changing their mind after purchase. If the product matches its listing and the customer's complaint is really about preference rather than accuracy or function, the representment should focus on demonstrating listing accuracy -- not relitigating whether the customer likes the product, which is not the standard the issuer applies.
